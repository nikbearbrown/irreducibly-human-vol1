## Table of Contents

### Theory Spine

**Preface** — The argument, the audience, the two books, and how to use this one

**Chapter 1 — The Microscope That Sits in a Box**
The opening argument. Every cognitive extension tool in human history extended
a specific capacity while requiring human judgment to direct it. AI is the
latest entry. A graduate who cannot audit AI output is a microscope sitting
in a box. Expensive. Useless. The book is about what it takes to produce the
scientist. Includes the author's own education as diagnostic case: the content
was never the problem; the irreplaceable parts never arrived.

**Chapter 2 — Why We Have Schools: Dewey Updated**
Dewey was right about method — active inquiry, learning by doing, reflective
thinking. He was wrong about purpose — growth for its own sake doesn't survive
a $200K price tag. The factory model had internal logic for 150 years and is
now training for skills that no longer pay the rent. The updated thesis: schools
exist to produce humans who can direct powerful tools toward human ends.

**Chapter 3 — Which Capacities Matter: The Seven Tiers**
The taxonomy that organizes human intelligence by what machines can and cannot
do. Not an academic classification — a triage. Tier 1 through Tier 7 with
educational implications at each level. Music, shop, PE, and ensemble work
as Tier 2 and 3 delivery mechanisms, not soft extras.

**Chapter 4 — The Companion Volume: *Intelligence?***
How to use *Intelligence?* — the natural history of cognition that establishes
operational definitions for each cognitive capacity and places AI on the same
evaluation axis used for every organism from bacteria to primates. The tier
taxonomy mapped onto the companion volume's chapters. The leitmotif named:
every extension note in *Intelligence?* points at the same structure; this
book starts where that argument ends.

**Chapter 5 — What School Is: The Experience That Cannot Be YouTubed**
There is nothing in a school that is not on YouTube in terms of facts. This is
also true of universities. What YouTube cannot teach: being wrong in front of
people and recovering, negotiating with someone you didn't choose, performing
for a real audience with no pause button, making things that can fail. The
homeschool question handled honestly. What a $200K education buys that YouTube
cannot provide.

**Chapter 6 — How to Read This Book: The Case Studies**
The editorial gate, the case template, what passes and what doesn't. How the
theory spine and case layer work together. How *Intelligence?* functions as
the reference spine for claims about specific capacities.

### Case Studies — Spring 2026 Edition

**Case 1 — The Junior Developer Pipeline: What the Software Industry's Hiring
Crisis Reveals About Which Capacities Actually Matter**
*Tiers 1→4 · Industry structural*

Between 2022 and 2025, entry-level developer postings dropped 60%. The cause
is not mysterious: AI eliminated the economic return on compliance and recall
at the code generation layer. The Microsoft preceptorship model proposes a
three-stage developmental sequence — technical failure signal, systems failure
signal, specification failure signal — targeting Tier 4 plausibility auditing
as the destination. IBM's rewritten entry-level roles as the cautionary case:
what happens when the developmental sequence terminates at Layer 1 and calls
itself a career path.

**Case 2 — The Frictional Framework: Measuring the Struggle as Evidence of
Genuine Learning**
*All tiers · Assessment methodology*

The artifact has been decoupled from the process that used to produce it.
The Genuine Learning Probability framework proposes seven observable friction
components — temporal engagement pattern, error trajectory coherence, cross-
context transfer, uncertainty calibration, social knowledge texture, retrieval
strength decay signature, scaffolding response curve — as an independent
evidence stream for genuine learning. The arms race argument: gaming all seven
components simultaneously is essentially equivalent in cost to performing the
underlying cognitive work.

**Case 3 — Botspeak: Teaching AI Fluency as a Tier 4 Capacity**
*Tier 4 entry · Course design*

A graduate course that closes the gap between AI literacy (knowing which tools
to use) and AI fluency (understanding the cognitive nature of the entity you
are collaborating with). The Five Modes framework — Specification, Delegation,
Conversation, Discernment, Diligence — as a complete architecture for AI
collaboration. The AI Use Disclosure as primary assessment instrument. The
fabricated citation that opened the course: not a broken tool story, but a
story about the gap where professional liability lives.

**Case 4 — Conducting AI: Building the Five Supervisory Capacities Under
Adversarial Conditions**
*Tier 4 advanced · Course design*

The conductor does not play any instrument. They hold the whole performance
in mind while each section plays its part. Five supervisory capacities no
algorithm possesses: plausibility auditing, problem formulation, tool
orchestration, interpretive judgment, executive integration. The Plausibility
Audit terminal assessment — Claude functioning adversarially against the
student's supervisory analysis. The solve-verify asymmetry stated: AI capability
scaling deepens rather than closes the human supervisory requirement.

**Case 5 — Dewey in the Age of AI: Why the Most Important Education Philosopher
of the 20th Century Was Right About Method and Wrong About Purpose**
*All tiers · Theoretical framework*

Dewey was right about method. He was wrong about purpose. The factory model
gave a wrong answer to the triage question. Dewey's growth model evaded it.
AI made evasion expensive. The updated thesis: schools exist to produce humans
who can direct powerful tools toward human ends. How *Intelligence?* provides
the map that corrects Dewey's teleology without abandoning his pedagogy.
The triage, made explicit, tier by tier.

**Case 6 — Computational Skepticism for AI: Teaching the Full Validation
Pipeline as a Cognitive Discipline**
*Tiers 4+5 integrative · Course design*

The most dangerous AI failure is not the one that crashes visibly. It is the
one that produces a plausible answer to the wrong question. INFO 7375 at
Northeastern University: fourteen weeks, six parts, Pearl's Ladder threaded
deliberately from observation through governance counterfactual. The Frictional
Journal and Changelog History as process evidence documentation. The Calibration
Baseline as the course's honest self-assessment: are students becoming better
at knowing what they know?

**Case 7 — Causal Reasoning: Teaching the Identification Layer AI Cannot Perform**
*Tier 5 · Course design*

Multiple distinct causal structures can be perfectly consistent with the same
statistical data. What resolves the ambiguity is domain knowledge — the engineer
who says "I know from operating this system for fifteen years that Y causes Z"
is providing something no dataset contains. The identification layer: DAG
construction, confounders, mediators, colliders, the backdoor criterion,
sensitivity analysis. The three specific identification decisions only the
human makes.

**Case 8 — Ethical Play: Teaching Felt Moral Weight as a Design Problem**
*Tier 3 · Course design*

An AI given a game design document can identify which ethical framework is
encoded in the mechanics. What it cannot do is feel implicated. The
implication/smugness distinction as the course's primary evaluative tool:
implication transfers moral responsibility to the player; smugness describes
what the player should feel. *Papers Please* as the canonical case. The
terminal deliverable: one sentence distinguishing a system that produces moral
weight from one that describes it.

**Case 9 — AImagineering: Teaching the Design Pipeline When Making Is No
Longer the Bottleneck**
*Tiers 3, 4, 7 · Course design*

Design thinking courses have always had a dirty secret: most of the semester
is spent making the artifact. AI eliminated that excuse. The missing stage:
the Commit — a specific course of action, with its evidential basis stated,
its uncertainty acknowledged, and its accountability owned. No AI tool makes
a Commit. The five required elements of the Commit document.

**Case 10 — Embodied Teaching: Drawing the Line Between What AI Handles and
What Only a Body Can Do**
*Tier 2 · Course design*

Every AI integration mandate issued to teachers in the last three years was
written for the wrong teacher. It fails for the woodshop teacher who hears the
blade catch wrong before the student does. The protected core as operational
specification across fifteen embodied domains. The distinction between "AI
cannot do this" and "AI has not yet done this" as the course's central
analytical instrument.

**Case 11 — The AI Sherpa: Building the Infrastructure That Converts
Consequential Experience into Practical Wisdom**
*Tier 7 · Practitioner handbook*

A student completes a six-month co-op at a Boston financial services firm.
Well-evaluated. Offered a return position. Her co-op coordinator asks what she
learned. She says: "I got really good at Excel modeling." This is not a student
failure. It is a design failure. The Sherpa relationship, the MVAL protocol,
the refiguration phase. Five field guides: co-op, study abroad, clinical
placement, trades apprenticeship, corporate early career.

## What This Book Is

*Irreducibly Human* is a graduate-level practitioner text organized around a
seven-tier taxonomy of human cognitive capacities, sorted by what AI can and
cannot do. It argues that the factory-model curriculum — designed to develop
the pattern recognition and recall that once commanded economic value — now
trains students to compete with machines at the machines' strongest capacities.
The book makes the case for what to teach instead, and how to know whether it
is working.

The book has two components:

**The theory spine** — stable across editions. Covers the tier taxonomy, the
Dewey argument updated for the AI era, the institutional argument for schools,
and the assessment infrastructure required to measure what matters.

**The case study layer** — rotating. Each edition's cases document real attempts
to develop specific tier capacities, with assessments, results, and honest
failure modes. Cases that pass the editorial gate ship in the edition under
the author's byline.

This structure is borrowed from *Causal Inference with Case Studies*
(Bear Brown LLC, Spring 2026). The structural bet is the same: practitioners
learn from documented real applications, not abstract frameworks.

## The Seven Tiers

**Tier 1 — Pattern & Association** · *AI capability: Superhuman*
Linguistic intelligence, logical-mathematical reasoning, recall, associative
lookup. Where standardized tests live. Where machines are now stronger than
any human who has ever lived. The correct educational response: teach students
to operate these tools, evaluate their outputs, and supply what the tools
cannot. Number sense, not long division.

**Tier 2 — Embodied & Sensorimotor** · *AI capability: Weak / emerging*
The knowledge that lives in the body: proprioception, physical skill, tool use,
the auditory and tactile feedback through a tool, the surgeon's hands, the
carpenter's feel for grain. Nobody stopped teaching woodshop because a language
model can describe joinery.

**Tier 3 — Social & Personal** · *AI capability: Simulates — doesn't feel*
Interpersonal intelligence, emotional regulation, moral reasoning under genuine
stakes. Machines produce outputs that read as empathetic without experiencing
anything. The danger is not that the output is wrong. The danger is that the
capacity atrophies in the person who stops exercising it.

**Tier 4 — Metacognitive & Supervisory** · *AI capability: Poor*
Plausibility auditing. Problem formulation. Tool orchestration. Interpretive
judgment. Tier 1 without Tier 4 is a very efficient way to be confidently wrong
at scale.

**Tier 5 — Causal & Counterfactual** · *AI capability: Weak to absent*
Pearl's ladder: observation, intervention, counterfactual. Current AI is
superhuman at Rung 1 and nearly absent at Rungs 2 and 3. A system that
identifies correlations cannot tell you whether your intervention will work.

**Tier 6 — Collective & Distributed** · *AI capability: Absent by definition*
Intelligence not possessed by any individual but emergent from systems of
people in relationship. Language models reflect the record of collective human
intelligence. They are absent from the practice that generated it.

**Tier 7 — Existential & Wisdom** · *AI capability: Absent — no stakes*
Phronesis. Practical wisdom. Requires stakes, the possibility of loss, a life
that can be poorly lived. An algorithm has no stakes. It cannot commit because
it cannot lose. Not a course — the horizon the series points toward.

## Editorial Gate

Cases pass two separate gates.

**Assignment gate (course credit):** A real analysis applying the tier framework
to a real context. All template sections present. Minimum source count met.
Explicit connection to at least one tier. Submitted as a pull request by
the deadline.

**Acceptance standard (publication):** Sources primary and verifiable. Methods
choices specific and attributed. Three case-layer outcomes supportable: a reader
can (a) reproduce the reasoning, (b) critique at least one assumption,
(c) apply the pattern to a different context. A failure mode is named — every
case, including success stories, must say where the approach fails or would
fail. Writing is tight. The case doesn't duplicate another in the edition.

Cases that pass ship under the author's byline. Cases that don't pass still
earn course credit if the assignment gate was cleared.
